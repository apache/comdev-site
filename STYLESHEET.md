# Stylesheet

## Titles and section headers

- Use _sentence case_ ('How this thing works') not _title case_ ('How This Thing Works'). In general, the more capital letters a text has, the harder it is to read quickly.

## Text

- The website default is US English.
- Write as if addressing the current reader ('Here you can find...') not an anonymous group ('Here people can find...')
- Avoid passive voice, which lots of readers, especially those for whom English is not their best language, find hard to follow. Passive voice also tends to blur who did what. Active voice holds reader attention better, and delivers information more effectively.
  - Passive voice: 'The decision was made that the service should be opened to all ASF committers.'
  - Active voice: 'Infra decided to open the service to all ASF committers.'
- Spell out acronyms on first use on a page. For this site, a lot of readers will not be familiar with terms ASF Members and committers take for granted.
  - Instead of 'TAC may be able to help you attend...'
  - Write 'The Travel Assistance Committee (TAC) may be able to help you attend...'
- Avoid colloquial terms that may be harder for people who do not live where you do to figure out.
  - Instead of 'If that does not work, try a Hail Mary:...' (US football term for a desperate move)
  - Write 'If that does not work, try this workaround:...'
- When describing website or application behavior, use present tense, not future tense.
  - Instead of 'When you submit the form, the site will take you to...'
  - Write 'When you submit the form, the site takes you to...'
- We are moving from using 'Apache' every time we talk about the foundation to using 'the ASF' more frequently.

## Charts and images

- Don't forget to add ALT text for people using assistive readers, or if the image does not come through properly.
