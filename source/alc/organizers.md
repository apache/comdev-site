Running an [ALC chapter](/alc/) is mostly about showing up, consistently,
for your local open source community. This page collects practical help
for chapter organizers.

{{% toc %}}

## Running events

The heart of a chapter is its events. These don't need to be large or
elaborate — a regular, reliable, welcoming meetup does far more for a
community than an occasional big conference.

* Start small and regular. A predictable monthly or quarterly cadence
  builds an audience.
* Mix formats: talks, workshops, hack sessions, and informal socials all
  have their place.
* Welcome newcomers explicitly. Many attendees will be students or people
  entirely new to open source.

## Finding speakers

You don't have to find every speaker yourself.

* Browse the ASF [speaker resources](/speakers/) and the list of
  [volunteer mentors and speakers](/contributors/asf-volunteers.html).
* Reuse and adapt our [sample presentations and slides](/speakers/slides.html)
  about Apache, the Apache Way, and open source.
* Invite local committers and users of Apache projects to share their
  experience.

## Topic ideas

* Introductions to the ASF and the Apache Way.
* "Getting started in open source" sessions aimed at students and
  first-time contributors (see our [newcomer guide](/newcomers/)).
* Deep dives on specific Apache projects used in your region.
* Non-code contribution: documentation, translation, design, events.

## Reporting

Chapters send a brief status report to ComDev every three months. Keep a
running note of your events and their turnout as you go — it makes the
quarterly report trivial to write, and it's useful material for your own
chapter's story.
